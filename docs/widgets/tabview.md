# Tab views

The widgets of [`ui/breeze/widgets_tabview.go`](../../ui/breeze/widgets_tabview.go), whose catalog entries are [`ui/breeze/catalog/tabview.json`](../../ui/breeze/catalog/tabview.json). What every widget shares, its platforms, themes and limits, is in [Breeze UI for MyGo](../breeze-ui.md).

## Looks

| Widget | macOS | Windows 11 | GNOME |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TabView` | the tab bar of a window's tabs: a 28-point capsule track 8 points in, equal tabs 24 high a point apart, the chosen one a white capsule, separators 1 by 16 between the others, the close button under the pointer at the leading edge, a 28-point + circle after the track | TabView: tabs 32 high, up to 240 wide each, 12-DIP headers, the chosen one SolidBackgroundFillColorTertiary with corners of 8 joined to the strip's line by 4-DIP curves, a 32 by 24 close button on every tab, separators between the others, the 32 by 24 add button after the tabs | AdwTabBar: a headerbar-colored bar 47 high, tabs 34 high sharing its width 5 apart in whole pixels, the chosen one currentColor at 10%, separators 28 high between the others, the round close button on the chosen tab and under the pointer, a flat new tab button at the end |

## Sources

`TabView` (`widgets_tabview.go`, with `TestScreenshotsTabView`) has no counterpart in package `ui`: it is a row holding `ui.TabsBase`'s tab list and a `ui.ButtonBase` adding a tab, each tab holding its title and a close button that is a button for the pointer and assistive technology but not a Tab stop. `TabView(c, &app.tab, app.titles, closeTab, newTab)` keeps `ui.Tabs`'s shape: the caller owns the titles and builds the chosen tab's content after the strip; a nil `close` leaves the tabs without close buttons and a nil `add` leaves out the add button. Before calling `close(i)` it moves the choice as the platforms do (to the tab after a closed chosen one, or before it when it was the last), so `close` only removes title `i`. Its values come from:

- **macOS:** the tab bar of NSWindow's tabbing, which [`appkit-reference`](../../tools/appkit-reference)'s tabs scene builds of three windows joined with `addTabbedWindow` (a delegate answering `newWindowForTab:` brings the + button) and lays out offscreen on macOS 27.2, printing its parts' frames: an NSTabBar 28 points high under the title bar, its track 8 points in from the window's sides, the tabs 24 high 2 points inside it and a point apart, equal in width, NSTabBarTrackInversionView separators 1 by 16 in those points, the + button 28 points square 4 after the track, and each tab's close button, by its accessibility frame ("Close"), 18 points square 4 in from the tab's leading edge. The bar is glass, which does not render offscreen; with `-live` the scene puts the window on the screen without activating the app and captures it (`screencapture -l`): the track is the label color at 9% (232 over white, 62 over the title bar's 42 in Dark Aqua) with a half-point rim, the chosen tab white (254, and 67 in Dark Aqua), the separators 204 and 85, the + circle 250 on average with a 1.3-point black plus 10.5 points across; the glass's edge, lighter at the top and darker at the sides, is drawn as a half-point rim (inferred). Breeze UI's strip matches the capture's sizes and fills; the capture's window is not the active app's, so it shows the titles dimmed, and Breeze UI draws them in labelColor and secondaryLabelColor (inferred). The close button shows only under the pointer: a capture taken with the pointer over an unchosen tab shows that tab's fill, 220 over the track's 232 (5%), and its close button's xmark, 6.85 points square in 0.9-point strokes; the close button's own circle under the pointer and the pressed tab are inferred. A tab bar belongs to a window's title bar; Breeze UI draws it where it is built, with the title bar's separator line below it.
- **Windows 11:** WinUI's TabView, microsoft-ui-xaml main at 5ed8dd3: `TabView_themeresources.xaml` (TabViewItemMinHeight 32, TabViewItemMaxWidth 240, TabViewItemHeaderFontSize 12, the header's and the close button's paddings and sizes, TabViewHeaderPadding, TabViewItemSeparatorMargin, the add button's size and container padding, and the brushes), `TabView.xaml` (the strip's columns and bottom lines, TabViewItem's states: the chosen tab semibold in TextFillColorPrimary, the others TextFillColorSecondary, LayerOnMicaBaseAltFillColorSecondary under the pointer), `TabViewItem.cpp` (UpdateTabGeometry, the chosen tab's shape with corners of OverlayCornerRadius and 4-DIP concave curves at its bottom, and UpdateCloseButton: CloseButtonOverlayMode Auto shows the close button on every tab, as Always does) and `TabView.cpp` (UpdateTabWidths' Equal mode, SetTabSeparatorOpacity hiding the separators of the chosen tab, of the one before it and around the one under the pointer, the KeyboardAccelerators Ctrl+F4, Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+Shift+Tab scoped to the TabView, and the tab chosen after one is removed). [`tools/winui-reference`](../../tools/winui-reference/Program.cs)'s `winui-tabview` scene renders two TabViews where `TestScreenshotsTabView` places Breeze UI's, at 2x on Windows 11 26H2 with WinUI 3 3.3, light and dark, and Breeze UI's strip now matches it but for the text (the host's font): the tabs' height and place 8 below the strip's top, the headers 8 into the chosen tab and 9 into the others (its TabContainer a DIP wider), the close buttons 36 and 37 DIPs before the tabs' ends, the separators 1 by 16 at the tabs' ends, hidden around the chosen one, the add button 3 after the tab column's footer and 3 above the strip's bottom, the chosen tab's fill (249 and 40), its corners and radius arcs, the line (229 and 29) and the header colors. The comparison corrected the tabs' start, 8 DIPs in (the LeftContentColumn's and the ScrollViewer's 2-DIP columns and the 4-DIP header), and their width: `UpdateTabWidths` shares out the TabView's width less the LeftContentColumn, the add button's column, the header and the footer, leaving the ScrollViewer's 2-DIP column out, so tabs that fill the strip are 161 DIPs wide in a 528-DIP TabView and the add button ends 2 DIPs past its end, where the strip's line stops at the TabView's end rather than running on under the button; five tabs without the add button (`winui-tabview-fill`) fill the strip the same way, their line also stopping at its end. It also corrected the chosen tab's outline, `CardStrokeColorDefault` laid over the tab's fill along its top (234 and 36) and over the window at its sides (229 and 29), which leaves the strip's line as it is where the radius arcs reach it, the close button's ×, whose ink is 8 DIPs square in strokes of three quarters of a DIP, and the add button's +, 10 DIPs across in the same strokes, both centered half a DIP above and before their buttons' middle (`TestFluentTabViewAsMeasured`).
- **GNOME:** libadwaita 1.10.0's AdwTabBar: `_tab-view.scss` (the bar's headerbar colors and shade, the tabbox's padding and min-height, the tab's 26-pixel min-height, 4-pixel padding and corners, its backgrounds, the separators' margins, the close button's 24-pixel round .flat face), `adw-tab.ui` and `adw-tab.c` (the title centered on the tab, the close button at its end, shown while the tab is chosen or under the pointer and taking no clicks while hidden), `adw-tab-box.c` (SPACING 5 between and around the tabs, which share the width with expand-tabs on, its default; the separators centered in that space, hidden by update_separators beside a chosen, hovered or pressed tab; the single-tab style) and `adw-tab-view.c` (its global shortcuts: Ctrl+Tab, Ctrl+Shift+Tab, Ctrl+Page Up, Ctrl+Page Down, Ctrl+Home, Ctrl+End and Alt+1 to Alt+0), with the end action's padding and `adw-tab-new.svg` for the add button. [`tools/adwaita-reference`](../../tools/adwaita-reference/reference.py)'s `tabview` set renders AdwTabBars where `TestScreenshotsTabView` places Breeze UI's, light and dark, with GTK 4.24.1, and Breeze UI's match it but for the glyphs: the bar's fill (255 and 46) and its shade (224 and 29), the tabs 6 below its top and 34 high, the chosen tab's fill (235 and 67), the separator 28 high (224 and 77), the close button's cross and the add button's icon and place. The comparison corrected the bar's height, 47 with the shade drawn over the box's bottom padding, where Breeze UI drew it below that padding and was 48; and the tabs' widths, which AdwTabBox gives in whole pixels (`adw_tab_box_size_allocate`: the room less SPACING around each tab divided and rounded up, a pixel taken from the first tabs until they fit, so 155, 156 and 156 in the test's bar), where Breeze UI's fractional widths put the tab edges, the close button and the separator half a pixel off; Breeze UI takes the width from the previous frame and lays the strip out once more when it changes. The second bar's tabs show a close button there, as AdwTabBar has no tabs that do not close, where Breeze UI's tab view built without `close` shows none. The close button under the pointer, the unchosen tab's fill under the pointer and pressed, and the separators hidden beside a pressed tab are not in the render: they are from the sources.

## Limits

Besides [those of every widget](../breeze-ui.md#limits):

- **Tab views.** Tabs do not reorder: dragging a tab and GNOME's Ctrl+Shift+Page Up, Page Down, Home and End, which move the chosen tab, would need a callback moving the caller's titles, which the minimal API leaves out. A middle click does not close a tab, as WinUI's and libadwaita's do: MyGo reports the middle button only to an element's HandleInput, which its Tester cannot press, so it is left out rather than untested. Where the tabs do not fit, they shrink below WinUI's TabViewItemMinWidth (100) instead of scrolling with WinUI's scroll buttons or AdwTabBar's scrolling. On Windows tabs that fill the strip run 2 DIPs past its end, as WinUI's do. The keys switching tabs work anywhere in the window when the focus is not in the strip, where WinUI scopes them to the TabView with its content, which Breeze UI does not hold; with two tab views the first enabled one built takes them. A key the tab view would not act on, such as Ctrl+Tab with a single tab, goes on to the window's other shortcuts. Ctrl+Home and Ctrl+End work on GNOME only with the focus in the strip, as in the window they would take the keys from text fields, where GTK lets the focused text take them first. What `TabView` returns is the strip, whose `Changed` reports nothing, as the tab list inside it is not reachable: read `*selected`. On GNOME the title is centered between room for the close button at both ends and ends in an ellipsis, where AdwTab centers it across the whole tab and fades it under the close button; WinUI clips a long header where Breeze UI ends it in an ellipsis.
