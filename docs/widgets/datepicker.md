# Date pickers

The widgets of [`ui/breeze/widgets_datepicker.go`](../../ui/breeze/widgets_datepicker.go), whose catalog entries are [`ui/breeze/catalog/datepicker.json`](../../ui/breeze/catalog/datepicker.json). What every widget shares, its platforms, themes and limits, is in [Breeze UI for MyGo](../breeze-ui.md).

## Looks

| Widget | macOS | Windows 11 | GNOME |

| `DatePicker` | the field of SwiftUI's compact DatePicker (NSDatePicker's textField bezel, 23 high, the date 3.5 in), opening the graphical calendar in a panel below it | CalendarDatePicker: a button's face 32 high, the date 12 in and the calendar glyph in a 32-DIP column, `CalendarView` in a flyout centered below it, closing as a day is chosen | GtkMenuButton: a raised button with the date and pan-down, a GtkCalendar without its border in a popover centered right below it, as GtkPopover places one without its arrow |

## Sources

`CalendarView` and `DatePicker` (`widgets_datepicker.go`, with `TestScreenshotsDatePicker`) have no counterpart in package `ui`, whose `Calendar` and `DateInput` they leave as they are: the calendar is a column of MyGo's elements whose grid of days is one focusable element, a table for assistive technology that names the day the keys are on with `Element.ActiveDescendant`, its days buttons named by their dates and checked where chosen, and the picker a `ui.ButtonBase` opening it with `ui.PopoverBase`. The names of the months and the weekdays, the dates, the numbers and the first day of the week come from MyGo's exported `i18n` package (`Months`, `Weekdays`, `FormatDate`, `FormatNumber`) in the locale MyGo's own controls write dates in, the window's language (`Context.Locale`) with the user's regional formats (`Context.Region`, `Region.FormatLocale`), so they follow the user without MyGo's internal translations. The header buttons' names, which no exported translation holds, are English strings of Breeze UI's own catalog, a library catalog of namespace `breeze` (`i18n.NewLibraryCatalog`, `ui/breeze/i18n/en.json`): an app translates or changes them with keys `breeze.<key>` in its own registered catalogs, as it changes MyGo's strings with `mygo.<key>` (`breeze.calendarPreviousMonth`, `calendarNextMonth`, `calendarPreviousYear`, `calendarNextYear`, `calendarToday`, `calendarPrevious`, `calendarNext`, and `datePickerPlaceholder`, the picker's text without a date). Their values come from:

- **macOS:** NSDatePicker of style clockAndCalendar, which SwiftUI's graphical DatePicker draws on macOS (the two render alike, 139 by 148 points), and NSDatePicker of style textField with `presentsCalendarOverlay`, the field of SwiftUI's compact DatePicker without its stepper, rendered at 2x in Aqua and Dark Aqua in a key window on macOS 27.2 by the datepicker scene of [`appkit-reference`](../../tools/appkit-reference) (`renderDatePicker`), once unfocused and once the window's first responder, at the places `TestScreenshotsDatePicker` puts Breeze UI's: the field and its lines, the header's title and its three buttons (their accessibility frames: ◀ and ▶ 6 by 7 points, ● 7, which AppKit leaves unnamed), the day names' row and the separator under it, the 18.5 by 18 cells with the numbers at their ends, the 18 by 16 selection with corners of 2.5 in `selectedContentBackgroundColor` with the focus and `unemphasizedSelectedContentBackgroundColor` without, today's number (#0060e5, #26a0ff) and the label colors over the field. The text sizes (13-point bold title, 10-point bold day names, 10-point numbers with proportional digits) are read from the glyphs' heights and widths. The overlay's panel is a window of its own that the renders do not show: it is drawn as a menu's panel (inferred).

[`tools/winui-reference`](../../tools/winui-reference/Program.cs)'s winui-datepicker scene places WinUI's at the test's places, its winui-datepicker-rtl scene places them right to left, and its winui-datepicker-open scene opens a CalendarDatePicker's flyout where `TestScreenshotsFluentDatePickerOpen` places Breeze UI's open picker (the flyout kept in the off-screen window, where WinUI centers it 4 DIPs below the picker, read and copied into the scene, as RenderTargetBitmap does not draw popups); both were rendered at 2x with WinUI 3 on Windows 11 26H2, light and dark, on the 6th of November 2026, the day the test fixes as today, so both mark the same day. Compared pixel by pixel with Breeze UI's, the day items, today's circle, the chosen day's ring, the text colors (the days of other months included), the calendar's border, background and corners, the picker's face and border, and the flyout's place and size (300 by 352, centered, 4 below) matched; the HeaderButton at rest is its text alone, as Breeze UI draws it. ## Limits

Besides [those of every widget](../breeze-ui.md#limits):

- **Date pickers.** WinUI's header button, which zooms out to a year's months and a decade's years, is the header's text, and the month view does not scroll on through the months or animate as CalendarView's does; CalendarView's several chosen days, blackout days and least and greatest dates, and NSDatePicker's and GtkCalendar's least and greatest dates, are not provided. On GNOME the arrows moving onto a day of the month before or after show that month, where GtkCalendar keeps its grid until Space chooses the day, and Page Up, Page Down, Home and End move as MyGo's calendar does, which GtkCalendar does not take. The calendar marks the day of the frame it was built in as today: past midnight the mark moves with the next frame. The buttons' names are English unless the app translates Breeze UI's `breeze.*` keys, where GTK and WinUI say theirs in the user's language; AppKit gives NSDatePicker's three buttons no names, so Breeze UI's are its own. macOS's calendar overlay and GNOME's popover arrow are not drawn as the platforms draw them: the overlay's panel is a menu's, and MyGo's popovers have no arrow, so GNOME's popover sits where GtkPopover puts one without its arrow, at the button's edge, 12 pixels higher than a GtkMenuButton's own with its arrow. MyGo draws a popover inside its window and moves it in from the window's edge, where GTK lets a popover's surface run past it (in the datepicker sets' 560-pixel window GTK's runs 8 pixels past the edge, Breeze UI's stops inside it). GtkCalendar takes the focus itself, before its four buttons, so Tab goes from the widget before it to the days and then to the buttons (the datepickerreopen set prints it: the popover opened has the calendar focused, and Tab goes on to Previous Month, Next Month, Previous Year and Next Year), where Breeze UI's buttons come before the days, as MyGo's Tab order is the elements' order and the days' table, the element taking the keys, follows the header. GtkCalendar keeps the place of the focused label in the grid (`focus_row`, `focus_col`) when the month changes, so after the header's buttons the ring stays on the same cell, where Breeze UI's stays on the day the keys are on, in the month shown. The renders of `tools/winui-reference` and `tools/adwaita-reference` mark the machine's today, which the test fixes as the 6th of November 2026 (the WinUI render was made that day). On Windows the day names' columns follow WinUI's widening only where nothing limits the calendar's width, as on a Canvas or in the picker's flyout; where its container gives WinUI's CalendarView a width, WinUI wraps a name that does not fit its 42-DIP column onto two lines instead, which Breeze UI does not draw. The flyout's shadow is MyGo's blur, wider than WinUI's.

## What MyGo would need

Besides [what every widget would need](../breeze-ui.md#what-mygo-would-need):

- **Dates.** A clock a `ui.Tester` starts from (`Tester.SetNow`), so that a test fixes the day a calendar marks as today without a hook of Breeze UI's own; exported translations of the calendar's buttons ("Previous month", "Next month"), so that `CalendarView` names them in the user's language as `Calendar` does without an app's own `breeze.*` strings; an arrow for `PopoverBase`, pointing at its anchor, as GtkPopover and NSPopover draw one; popovers in surfaces of their own, which may run past the window's edge as GtkPopover's do, rather than moved in from it; and a Tab order apart from the elements' order, or an element that takes the keys before the controls it holds without being the days' table, so that GtkCalendar's days come before its buttons as GTK's do.
