# Text

The widgets of [`ui/breeze/widgets_text.go`](../../ui/breeze/widgets_text.go), whose catalog entries are [`ui/breeze/catalog/text.json`](../../ui/breeze/catalog/text.json). What every widget shares, its platforms, themes and limits, is in [Breeze UI for MyGo](../breeze-ui.md).

## Looks

| Widget | macOS | Windows 11 | GNOME |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Text` | the text styles of `NSFont.preferredFont(forTextStyle:)`: largeTitle 26 points, title1 22, title2 17, headline 13 bold, body 13, caption1 10; secondary text in `secondaryLabelColor` | the TextBlock styles: TitleLarge 40, Title 28, Subtitle 20, BodyStrong 14 (semibold), Body 14, Caption 12; secondary text in `TextFillColorSecondary` | `.title-1` (181%, weight 800), `.title-2` (136%, 800), `.title-4` (118%, bold), `.heading`, a plain label, `.caption` (82%, line height 140%); secondary text `.dimmed` (55% opacity) |

`Text(c, s, style...)` draws a text in a step of the platform's type ramp: `TextLargeTitle`, `TextTitle`, `TextSubtitle`, `TextHeading`, `TextBody` or `TextCaption`, with `TextSecondary` added by `|` to dim any of them (`breeze.Text(c, "Applies to every window.", breeze.TextCaption|breeze.TextSecondary)`). It is `ui.Text` with the step's size, weight and line height, in the theme's font. The style comes after the text and can be left out: without one it is `TextBody`, which draws exactly what `ui.Text` draws, so `breeze.Text(c, s)` replaces `ui.Text(c, s)` as Breeze UI's other widgets replace theirs. The steps are the ones the three platforms share: a strong body, WinUI's BodyStrong, is `TextHeading`, as macOS's headline and libadwaita's `.heading` are the body in bold too; the subtitle is libadwaita's `.title-4`, since `.title-3` is `.title-2`'s size; libadwaita's `.body`, which only raises a paragraph's line height to 140%, is left to `LineHeight(1.4)`. Dimming is a flag rather than more steps because each platform dims any style the same way: AppKit with `secondaryLabelColor`, WinUI with `TextFillColorSecondary`, both the theme's `TextMuted`, and libadwaita by the label's opacity (`.dimmed`), which Breeze UI sets as `Opacity` on GNOME. A title is plain text to assistive technology, as the platforms' title styles are; `Role(ui.RoleHeading)` and `Level` make it a heading. The name stays `Text` rather than `Label`, which would read as `Element.Label`, the accessible name that Breeze UI's examples set everywhere.

## Sources

`Text`'s steps (`widgets_text.go`, with `TestScreenshotsText`) come from the platforms' type ramps:

- **macOS:** `NSFont.preferredFont(forTextStyle:)` read on macOS 27.2: each style's size, weight (the font's `wght` axis: 700 for headline, 400 for the others used) and line height (`NSLayoutManager.defaultLineHeight`), and labels in those fonts rendered at 2x by `appkit-reference` in the places of the screenshot test, whose glyphs Breeze UI's match in size and width, the lines a little shorter (see Limits), so the glyphs half a point lower but the caption's, and 2 points further left, as an NSTextField label insets its text by 2. The labels' text is darker there than their colors (labelColor's 85% black reads 6 at the glyphs' cores where Breeze UI's reads 39, secondaryLabelColor's 50% reads 64 where Breeze UI's reads 128, and in Dark Aqua 250 and 209 where Breeze UI's read 221 and 154): an NSTextField drawn by `cacheDisplay` lays its color's alpha on twice, as a label in a static 50% black reads 63 too while the same font drawn in labelColor by `NSString.draw` reads 39, so the text keeps the label colors' own values (the theme's `Text` and `TextMuted`, `mac.go`) rather than the render's.
- **Windows 11:** `TextBlock_themeresources.xaml` of microsoft-ui-xaml's main branch: the `FontSize` and `FontWeight` of `TitleLargeTextBlockStyle`, `TitleTextBlockStyle`, `SubtitleTextBlockStyle`, `BodyStrongTextBlockStyle`, `BodyTextBlockStyle` and `CaptionTextBlockStyle`, all based on `BaseTextBlockStyle` (SemiBold, `LineStackingStrategy` MaxHeight, no `LineHeight` and no `CharacterSpacing`; Subtitle, Title and TitleLarge add `OpticalMarginAlignment` TrimSideBearings, see Limits), and `TextFillColorSecondary` from `Common_themeresources_any.xaml`. `winui-reference` renders these TextBlocks where `TestScreenshotsText` places Breeze UI's Text (`winui-text`) at 2x on Windows 11 26H2, light and dark: the sizes and weights are the styles', and the colors match, the text 26 and 255 and the secondary text 92 and 204 over the window. The boxes differ by the font's line spacing (WinUI's lines are 53.2, 37.2, 26.6, 18.6 and 16 DIPs high for 40, 28, 20, 14 and 12 pixels of Segoe UI Variable) and the titles' first glyph by TrimSideBearings, which Breeze UI leaves out (see Limits); nothing was corrected.
- **GNOME:** libadwaita 1.10.0's `_labels.scss` (`.title-1` to `.title-4`, `.heading`, `.body`, `.caption`, `.dimmed`, no letter spacing) and `--dim-opacity` in `_colors.scss` (55%, 90% with higher contrast); `.large-title` and `.dim-label` are deprecated there in favor of `.title-1` and `.dimmed` (`_deprecated.scss`). `reference.py`'s `text` set renders these labels where `TestScreenshotsText` places Breeze UI's, light and dark, with GTK 4.24.1 and Adwaita Sans: the glyphs' heights match within a pixel at 2x for every step (the large title's capitals 50 pixels at 2x, the caption's 21 to 22), and so do the text colors, 50 and 255 at the glyphs' cores and the dimmed text's 140 and 155.

## Limits

Besides [those of every widget](../breeze-ui.md#limits):

- **Type ramp.** MyGo lays out a line as the font's ascender and descender, without the leading AppKit adds, so on macOS every text, `ui.Text`'s and `Text`'s, is a little shorter than AppKit's line height (15.3 points for the body's 16, 30.6 for the large title's 32); `Text` keeps MyGo's spacing on every step so that the body stays `ui.Text`'s. On Windows MyGo draws Segoe UI (its `system-ui` in `dwrite_windows.go`) rather than Segoe UI Variable, WinUI's font on Windows 11, and so without that font's optical sizes: the glyphs differ a little in shape and width, not in size. WinUI's Subtitle, Title and TitleLarge trim their first glyph's side bearing (`OpticalMarginAlignment` TrimSideBearings), which MyGo cannot, so they start that bearing further right than WinUI's. WinUI's Display (68) and BodyLarge (18) steps, macOS's title3, callout, subheadline and footnote and libadwaita's `.title-3`, `.caption-heading`, `.document`, `.monospace` and `.numeric` have no step of their own; `FontSize`, `FontWeight`, `Font("monospace")` and `FontFeatures("tnum")` on the returned text give them.
